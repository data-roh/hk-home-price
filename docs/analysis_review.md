# Review of the Real-Data Analysis

Numbers below match `notebooks/01_home_model_reconstruction.ipynb` and `outputs/` as regenerated by `run_research_audit.py`.

## Conclusion

The empirically supported scope is the **Home price model**. The supplied BQNT monthly file contains the Home target and the five disclosed predictors, with 199 complete months from 2010-02 to 2026-08. Some Office predictors are present, but the target `office_rent` is missing, so the Office model is not estimated.

Three findings drive the design:

1. **The level relation is not cointegrated.** Prices and most predictors are I(1). Engle–Granger on the level regression gives p ≈ 0.98, and the ECM gate (log levels) gives p ≈ 0.92. The level GLSAR is therefore kept as a reconstruction of the BI model family, not as a long-run equilibrium.
2. **The level GLSAR(1) is effectively a regression on monthly changes.** $\hat\rho \approx 0.993$ (half-life ≈ 100 months), innovation s.d. ≈ 3.84 index points. Its innovations fail Ljung–Box (p ≈ $10^{-16}$) and ARCH LM (p ≈ $10^{-8}$). AR(2) errors do not fix this.
3. **A growth-rate model with momentum is the better short-run specification.** It makes no long-run level claim, cleans most of the residual autocorrelation, and gives the best-calibrated intervals. It still does not beat a random walk beyond one month.

None of the results reproduce BI's coefficients or forecasts, which were never available.

## Models

| Model | Specification | Key estimates |
|---|---|---|
| Level GLSAR(1) (`baseline`) | $y_t = a + X_t\beta + u_t$, $u_t = \rho u_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$, feasible GLS | Fed −8.4 pts per 1pp; secondary sales +1.2 pts per 1,000 deals; rent +1.1 pts per index point (all p < 0.001); HSI and CNYHKD p > 0.5; max VIF 5.7 |
| Growth (`growth`) | $\Delta\log y_t = c + \phi\Delta\log y_{t-1} + \Delta X_t\gamma + \varepsilon_t$, OLS with Newey–West (6 lags) | $\phi$ ≈ 0.61; Fed −0.012 per pp (p ≈ 0.001); Δlog sales +0.022 (p < 0.001); Δlog rent +0.105 (p ≈ 0.29); $R^2$ ≈ 0.59; residual s.d. ≈ 0.99% per month |

Fed rate (−) and transactions (+) are robust across both models. Rent is significant only in levels, so its level link is most likely a shared trend.

## Scenario: Fed −100bp, permanent, other X at last value

| vs. baseline | Month 1 | Month 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Level GLSAR | +2.6% | +2.6% |
| Growth model | +1.2% | +3.1% (long-run $-\hat\gamma_{fed}/(1-\hat\phi)$ ≈ 3.08%) |

Same direction and similar size, different timing. These are conditional projections. Under the HKD peg the Fed rate is not exogenous to Hong Kong, so they are not causal effects. No scenario input falls outside the training range.

## Out-of-sample comparison

Protocol (`PSEUDO_OOS` in `src/hkproperty/validation.py`, shared by the notebook, the audit script, and the app):

- expanding window starting at month 120, re-fitted every 3 months;
- horizons 1, 3, 6, and 12;
- future X held at the last training value (no oracle X);
- all models scored only on origins where every model succeeded. No origin failed.

### Method challengers (same five predictors): 23 common origins, 2020-01 to 2025-07

RMSE in index points:

| Model | h=1 | h=3 | h=6 | h=12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level GLSAR(1) | 5.46 | 11.54 | 19.66 | 31.22 |
| Growth (Δlog + momentum) | **4.53** | 10.74 | 21.93 | 41.17 |
| AR(2) errors | 5.91 | 13.13 | 21.06 | 32.13 |
| Fourier K=1 | 5.22 | 10.69 | 19.27 | 31.18 |
| X lagged one month | 7.11 | 13.55 | 21.47 | 33.34 |
| Logs | 6.38 | 13.20 | 21.34 | 33.18 |
| Random walk | 5.38 | **10.43** | **17.35** | **28.10** |
| Seasonal naive | 26.49 | 26.54 | 26.56 | 28.10 |

95% interval coverage (target 95%):

| Model | h=1 | h=3 | h=6 | h=12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level GLSAR(1) | 74% | 70% | 74% | 43% |
| Growth | 87% | 91% | 83% | 70% |
| AR(2) | 78% | 74% | 70% | 48% |
| Fourier K=1 | 78% | 74% | 61% | 39% |

Reading:

- The growth model is the only one that beats the random walk at h = 1 (−16% RMSE). It overshoots at turning points at longer horizons. Damped momentum is the next refinement.
- No GLSAR variant beats the random walk at 3–12 months. Fourier K=1 is the best GLSAR variant but only marginally, so seasonality is not the bottleneck.
- AR(2): $\hat\rho_1+\hat\rho_2$ ≈ 0.99, Ljung–Box still fails, and forecasts get worse. Lagged X and logs are also worse.
- GLSAR intervals are too narrow. The growth model's are much closer to nominal.
- 23 overlapping origins support an indicative ranking only. Diebold–Mariano tests are the next step.

### Transaction extensions (common 2011+ sample): 19 common origins, 2020-12 to 2025-06

| Model | RMSE h=1 | h=3 | h=6 | h=12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline_2011` | 4.24 | 12.59 | 20.25 | 33.52 |
| `alt_secondary` (Land Registry secondary agreements replace Midland) | 4.24 | 12.58 | 20.23 | 33.47 |
| `transactions_plus` (+ total residential agreements) | 4.24 | 12.60 | 20.28 | 33.62 |
| `value_plus` (+ residential consideration value) | 4.24 | 12.59 | 20.29 | 33.73 |
| Random walk | **4.09** | **11.98** | **18.88** | **30.90** |

The new variables are significant in sample (p ≈ 0.02 and 0.001), but out-of-sample RMSE moves only in the second decimal and all four lose to the random walk. Land Registry secondary agreements correlate 0.99 with Midland secondary sales. Adding them cuts the Midland coefficient from 2.38 to 0.79, so they are substitutes, not complements. None is promoted. Midland can be swapped for the public Land Registry series at no cost.

## Variable integration decisions

`run_research_audit.py` classifies every extension column by overlap with the Home sample, monthly contiguity, and role. Results are in `outputs/variable_integration_audit.csv`:

| Decision | Columns | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 6 | Disclosed Home variables |
| `candidate_x` | 4 | Land Registry residential/primary agreements and values, 2011+; two are tested in notebook 01 §7.2 |
| `replace_and_compare` | 1 | Land Registry secondary agreements vs. Midland |
| `alternate_transform` | 2 | MoM/YoY versions, used only in change-based specifications |
| `validation_y` | 1 | RVD All Classes price index: same concept as the target, so never a predictor |
| `short_sample_only` | 2 | CSB job-board postings, 2020+ (≈ 80 months), too short for the 120-month protocol |
| `defer` | 2 | Restaurant licence stock and government job ads: internal missing months, so they need a design for incomplete snapshots |
| `availability_flag` | 3 | Completeness flags, not economic variables |
| `not_usable` | 8 | Lever single-employer series (crypto, Lalamove): one month, after the target ends |

Additional rules:

- Quarterly vacancy and annual labour statistics stay at their native frequency. Forward-filling them to monthly without publication dates would leak future information.
- The Land Registry detail block (134 wide columns) is too wide for the sample. Screen housing-relevant definitions first, then use grouped reduction or tight ablations.

## Next steps

Method:

1. Damped momentum in the growth model.
2. Diebold–Mariano tests on the common origins.
3. Johansen and Gregory–Hansen (break-robust) cointegration tests.
4. Empirical intervals based on out-of-sample errors.

Data, in order (notebook 01 §9):

1. Government policy stance: event layer plus a surprise or text-tone measure.
2. Foreign finance and legal firms: Grade A office data and a fixed-panel hiring series, tested against large units (RVD Classes D–E) and rent.
3. District restaurant panel against regional price indices.
4. HIBOR, mortgage applications/approvals/drawdowns, and release-date (vintage) information.

Each new variable enters the growth model as a change, one channel at a time, and must beat both the growth model and the random walk on common origins.
